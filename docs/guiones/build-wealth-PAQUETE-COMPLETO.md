## 0) QUÉ CAMBIÓ vs el guion anterior (las 3 críticas, resueltas)
1. **Emoción/historia:** el largo abre con DOS personajes (Marcus vs Dylan), no con lógica. La gente recuerda personas, no porcentajes.
2. **Autoridad en el primer minuto:** sin cara, la autoridad viene de precisión + nombrar el error que todos cometen. Añadido en SEG 1.
3. **Curiosity gap cada ~20s:** sembrados open loops explícitos ("the third one nobody expects", "there's a catch", "the number that made me angry").

### SEG 3 · WHAT WEALTH ACTUALLY IS (1:25–2:15)
> *"Here's where almost everyone gets stuck. Wealth is not a paycheck — a paycheck stops the second you stop working. Wealth is owning things that pay you whether you show up or not.*
> *Dylan bought little machines. You buy them once, and they keep working after you clock out — while you sleep, while you're on vacation, while you do absolutely nothing. Your job pays you for your time. An asset pays you for your ownership. That one word — ownership — is the entire game. And there are only three machines that matter."*

`[Open loop: "only three machines" → promete la lista.]`

### SEG 4 · THE 3 ASSETS + THE MATH (2:15–4:30) — CLÍMAX
> *"Ninety percent of self-made wealth comes down to three assets.*
> *One: index funds. Instead of betting on one company, you own a slice of the five hundred biggest at once. Historically, around seven percent a year after inflation. Boring — and boring is why it works.*
> *Two: real estate. And before you say you can't afford it — REITs let you own a piece of property for the price of a coffee. It pays you rent, and it rises with inflation instead of getting eaten by it.*
> *Three — and this is the one nobody expects — ownership. Equity. A slice of a business, or your own. Highest risk, highest ceiling. This is the one that turned Dylan from comfortable into wealthy.*
> *Now here's the number that made me angry I wasn't taught it in school. Same fifty… no — let's be fair, let's give Marcus's discipline to an asset. Five hundred a month. One index fund. Thirty years. Seven percent.*
> *You put in a hundred and eighty thousand dollars of your own money. But you don't walk away with a hundred and eighty thousand. You walk away with around six hundred thousand. Look at that gap — four hundred and twenty thousand dollars you never worked a single hour for. Your asset made it while you slept. THAT is what Marcus's discipline never did alone. Same discipline, plus an engine, and the whole story changes."*

`[La curva compuesta se DIBUJA en vivo — clímax visual + emocional. Resuelve el open loop de Marcus/Dylan.]`

### SEG 5 · WHERE HABITS DO WIN (4:30–5:20) — honestidad = confianza
> *"Now I'm not telling you habits are useless — that would be dumb, and anyone who tells you to burn your budget is selling something. Here's the honest version: habits are the fuel line to the engine. That five-dollar coffee, skipped and redirected into an asset, becomes almost eighteen thousand dollars in ten years. The habit didn't build that. The habit just fed the asset faster. So the rule is simple: a habit is only worth what it buys you in ownership."*

### SEG 6 · THE TRAP (5:20–6:20) — nuevo open loop
> *"But there's a catch, and it catches smart people the hardest. It's spending that feels like investing. The upgraded car you 'needed for work.' The three-hundred-dollar course you never finished. The gadget that was going to make you rich. They feel like progress. But an asset puts money into your pocket; a liability quietly takes it out — wearing the costume of an investment. So before you buy anything you're calling an investment, ask one question: does this pay me back, or do I pay for it forever? If you can't answer in one sentence, it's not an asset. It's a bill with better marketing."*

### SEG 7 · ONE MOVE + CLOSE + CTA (6:20–7:30)
> *"So here's your move this week — not someday, this week. Open one investment account. Automate one transfer into one broad index fund. Fifty dollars is enough. The amount isn't the point — owning your first machine is. Because the gap between Marcus and Dylan was never income, and it was never discipline. It was ownership.*
> *Habits make you disciplined. Assets make you rich. Confuse the two, and you become the most disciplined broke person you know. Don't be Marcus. Buy the engine.*
> *And if you want to know exactly which index fund Dylan would pick and why — I break down the three, side by side, in this video right here. I'll see you in that one."*

## 15) EL TEMPLATE REUTILIZABLE (v0 — a validar con este video)
Esqueleto de bloques que cualquier tema futuro rellena. Cada bloque tiene una FUNCIÓN, no un contenido:
```
[HOOK 0-5s]         misterio/contraste, cero intro           → "Two X. Same Y. Opposite result."
[AUTHORITY 5-15s]   nombrar el error que todos cometen        → "I broke down 100 of these..."
[STORY 15-60s]      personaje concreto = emoción              → Marcus vs Dylan
[REFRAME]           la idea que lo cambia todo                → "ownership is the game"
[PROOF + LIST]      números + lista numerada                  → "3 assets, 90% of wealth"
[VISUAL PAYOFF]     el gráfico que se dibuja = clímax          → la curva compuesta
[HONESTY BEAT]      concede algo = confianza                  → "habits aren't useless"
[OPEN LOOP / TRAP]  nueva curiosidad antes de decaer          → "a catch that catches smart people"
[ONE MOVE]          acción minúscula, baja fricción           → "this week: 1 account"
[CTA / SESSION]     callback + siguiente video                 → mantener la sesión
```
> Cuando la IA detecte un tema nuevo, rellena estos 10 bloques con el contenido del tema. Pero
> los PESOS de cada bloque (cuánto dura, cuánta emoción) se calibran con la retención real de
> los primeros videos. Template = motor; retención medida = combustible que lo afina.
