### SEG 1 — HOOK (0:00–0:20) · retener = prometer un enemigo + un secreto
> "You've been told to build wealth with better habits. Wake up early. Skip the five-dollar coffee. Track every dollar. And be honest — you've tried. So why are you still broke?
> Here's the uncomfortable truth almost nobody explains: **habits don't build wealth. Assets do.** And in the next few minutes I'm going to show you the exact difference — including the three assets that do about ninety percent of the work."

### SEG 2 — THE HABIT LIE (0:20–1:15) · nombrar al enemigo
> "There's an entire industry built on selling you habits. The 5 a.m. club. The no-latte rule. The color-coded budget. And look — none of it is *wrong*. But here's what they don't tell you: the most disciplined person on earth, saving fifty dollars a month under a mattress, will retire poor.
> Because habits, by themselves, only do one thing: they move money you already have from one pocket to another. They never *make* money. Discipline is the fuel. But fuel with no engine goes nowhere. **The engine is the asset.**"

*[B-roll: mattress with cash, hamster wheel; text: "FUEL ≠ ENGINE"]*
*Retención: valida ("none of it is wrong" = confianza, no eres un fanático) luego gira. "Retire poor" = stakes.*

### SEG 3 — WHAT WEALTH ACTUALLY IS (1:15–2:00) · la definición que cambia todo
> "So let's define it properly, because this is where most people get stuck. Wealth is not a big paycheck. A paycheck stops the second you stop working. **Wealth is owning things that pay you whether you show up or not.**
> Economists call these income-producing or appreciating assets. I'll call them what they are: little machines you buy once, that keep working after you clock out. Your job pays you for your time. An asset pays you for your ownership. That one word — *ownership* — is the whole game."

*[Visual: "TIME → paycheck (stops)" vs "OWNERSHIP → asset (doesn't)"]*
*Retención: re-hook ("this is where most get stuck") + una idea que reencuadra ("ownership") = "aha".*

### SEG 4 — THE 3 ASSETS + THE MATH (2:00–4:00) · el pico de valor
> "So which machines actually work? Roughly ninety percent of self-made wealth comes down to three.
> **One: index funds.** Instead of betting on one company, you own a tiny slice of the five hundred biggest ones at once. Historically, that basket has returned around seven percent a year after inflation. Boring. And boring is exactly why it works.
> **Two: real estate** — or if you don't have a down payment, REITs, which let you own a slice of property for the price of a coffee. It pays rent, and it tends to rise with inflation instead of getting eaten by it.
> **Three: ownership** — equity in a business. Your own side project, or shares of companies you believe in. The highest risk, and the highest ceiling.
> Now here's the part that should make you angry you weren't taught it in school. Watch what *one* of these does with a small, boring habit attached to it."

> "Five hundred dollars a month. A simple index fund. Thirty years. Around seven percent, historically. Over those thirty years you personally put in a hundred and eighty thousand dollars. But you don't end with a hundred and eighty thousand. You end with **around six hundred thousand.**
> Look at that gap. Four hundred and twenty thousand dollars you never worked for. Your asset made it — compounding, quietly, while you were asleep, on vacation, living your life. *That* is what a habit can never do alone, and what an asset does by design."

*[Visual: 3 icons build in. Then a compound curve animates: flat line of contributions vs exploding total. Label: "$180k in → ~$600k out · illustrative, ~7%/yr".]*
*Retención: lista numerada (cerebro adora listas) → "should make you angry" (emoción) → el gráfico compuesto ES el clímax visual.*

### SEG 5 — WHERE HABITS *DO* WIN (4:00–5:00) · honestidad = confianza
> "Now, I'm not telling you to throw away every good habit. That would be dumb. Here's the honest version: habits are not the enemy. They're the *fuel line* to the engine. Skipping that daily coffee doesn't build wealth — but redirected into an asset, that same five dollars a day becomes almost eighteen thousand dollars in ten years. The habit didn't do that. The habit just *fed the asset faster.*
> So the rule is simple: every habit is only worth what it buys you in ownership. Discipline that buys assets is a superpower. Discipline that just sits in a savings account, losing to inflation, is a trap."

*[Visual: coffee → arrow → asset icon → "$5/day → ~$18k/10yr"]*
*Retención: concede ("that would be dumb") = desarma al escéptico = confianza. Convierte el hábito en aliado, no lo tira.*

### SEG 6 — THE TRAP (5:00–6:00) · el giro que evita que se vayan
> "But there's a trap, and it catches smart people. It's spending that *feels* like investing. The upgraded car you 'needed for work.' The course you bought and never finished. The gadget that was going to make you money. These feel productive. They feel like progress. But an asset puts money *into* your pocket. A liability quietly takes it out — while wearing the costume of an investment.
> Before you buy anything you're calling an investment, ask one question: **does this pay me back, or do I pay for it forever?** If you can't answer that in one sentence, it's not an asset. It's a bill with better marketing."

*[Visual: "ASSET → $ in" vs "LIABILITY in disguise → $ out". Icons: car, course, gadget with tiny down-arrows.]*
*Retención: nuevo open loop ("catches smart people" = tú eres smart, ¿te atrapa?) + una pregunta-herramienta que se llevan.*

### SEG 7 — ONE MOVE + CLOSE (6:00–7:00) · pago final + acción
> "So here's your one move this week — not someday, this week. Open one investment account. Automate a single transfer into one broad index fund. It can be fifty dollars. The amount is not the point. Owning your first machine is the point. Because the gap between people who build wealth and people who just *budget* it isn't income, and it isn't discipline. It's ownership.
> Habits make you disciplined. Assets make you rich. Confuse the two, and you'll become the most disciplined broke person you know. Don't be that person. Buy the engine.
> If this reframed how you see your money, the deeper breakdown of those three assets is right here — and I'll see you in that one."

*[Visual: "THIS WEEK: 1 account · 1 auto-transfer · 1 index fund". End card + next video thumbnail.]*
